Healthcare and Insurance for Expats in Mexico in 2026

Quality, affordable healthcare is one reason expats choose Mexico. Private care here costs 50–70% less than in the US for comparable quality. Here's how healthcare works and what to choose in 2026.

Three options for an expat

The public IMSS system

IMSS (Instituto Mexicano del Seguro Social) is public healthcare. Residents with temporary or permanent status can enroll voluntarily for a fixed annual fee ($400–$700 depending on age). Pros: cheap and covers almost everything. Cons: queues, and some chronic conditions aren't covered in the first year.

Private care and insurance

Private hospitals in major cities are modern, with English- and Russian-speaking staff. A specialist visit is $40–$80 without insurance. Private insurance (GNP, AXA, Bupa) covers costly surgery and hospital stays; cost depends on age — from $1,000/year at 40, rising steeply after 65.

What to choose

  • Young and healthy: pay-as-you-go + basic accident insurance.
  • Families: private insurance with hospitalization cover.
  • Retirees: combine IMSS (cheap) + private for surgery. Insure early — premiums jump after 70.

Pharmacies and medicine

Medicine in Mexico is cheaper and often sold without a prescription. Pharmacy clinics (consultorios) offer visits from $3 — handy for simple cases.
