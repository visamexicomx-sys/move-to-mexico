10 most beautiful cities in Mexico – Places in Mexico

Mexico— The state is very large to cover all its interesting places at once. When planning a trip to Mexico, take into account our selection of the 10 most beautiful cities in Mexico.

Which city in Mexico do we like best? Or which ones do we suggest visiting? These are very difficult questions. If there is a prospect, visit everything in turn!

Our list is in alphabetical order, not in order of preference.

Aguascalientes / Aguascalientes

The city itself may not seem as charming as other cities on this list. But walking along its streets, looking at the frescoes of the government palace and delving a little deeper into simple neighborhoods, you begin to feel its special charm. Plaza Patria — central guest sovereign place in.

It's a good time to come to Aguascalientes — to visit the San Marcos Fair. But even without the Jardin de San Marcos fair — This is perhaps the best attraction in the city. The garden is full of cute squirrels who will greet you throughout your walk.

Ciudad de México (CDMX) / Mexico City

The capital of the state is huge, and in a huge metropolis it is difficult to choose just one thing that you will like. There are an endless number of places in Mexico City that will be remembered by you for their beauty, national flavor and historical value. Even a week is not enough to see even the main attractions of Mexico City. You need to come back here again and again.

In CDMX (as the federal district of Mexico is now called), you can visit the Chapultepec Castle, the Los Pinos Cultural Center (which was the home of the presidents), the Palace of Fine Arts, the historical center, and in the building of the Ministry of Education you can see the murals of Diego Rivera. The Museum of Anthropology and the Cathedral are also must-sees. And this list can be continued endlessly. It is the variety of choices that makes Mexico City one of the most beautiful cities in the country.

Guadalajara / Guadalajara

The pearl of Mexico, the capital of the state that gave us tequila and many Mexican traditions. How and Mexico City is a city where the number of historical places and monuments is simply off the charts. In the historical center, a must-see is the Plaza de Armas, the Degollado Theater, and several cathedrals.

A tour of the municipal market is also not to be missed. You may want to bring with you traditional Mexican clothing, textiles or beautiful handicrafts for which the state of Jalisco is so famous.

Guanajuato / Guanajuato

It's also the state capital, but without the feeling of a huge city. Its center is so rich in history, color and beauty that Guanajuato is considered almost the most Mexican city, a concentrate of Mexican traditions. Be sure to explore its alleys, starting with the famous Alley of Kisses. It's absolutely safe and very romantic!

In Guanajuad, in other words, there is a tradition: where students of music schools and groups simply take to the streets and give their music to all passersby. While walking around the city, go up to the Pipila monument, from here you can enjoy a beautiful view of the city — it's an explosion of colors.

And if you're brave enough, you can take a tour of the Guanajuato Mummies Museum, which has even appeared in films such as "The Saint of the Guanajuato Mummies."

Mérida / Mérida

The so-called «white city» — the capital of the state of Yucatan, is considered one of the safest cities in Mexico. Everything that surrounds the Plaza de Armas already deserves an absolute visit. One of the main historical symbols of Merida is the Casa de Montejo mansion, and in addition to this, the Palacio de Gobierno, within the walls of which today government offices of the state of Yucatan are located. Don't miss the free events offered by the city's travel office, such as the Vaqueria dance. The best place for walking is the historic Paseo de Montejo; at the end of the 19th century, the wealthiest townspeople built their luxurious mansions on this street. On the boulevard is the luxurious Palacio Canton, which is now home to the Regional Museum of Anthropology.

In addition, when coming to Merida, do not forget to try such masterpieces of Yucatecan cuisine as papadsoules, panuchos, cochinita pibil and others.

Morelia / Morelia

The city where the hero of Mexican Independence, Jose Maria Morelos y Pavon, was born. Having visited its historical center, we will see very nice beautiful churches, parks and the Rose Winter Garden. The Morelia Aqueduct appears on the 50-peso bill — you will definitely see him too.

It's also worth visiting the Michoacan Crafts Museum in a former monastery of San Francisco, where you'll be sure to buy something to remember the city.

Puebla / Puebla

The city is famous for its Battle of May 5, which ended in victory over French troops. Situated between four volcanoes, Puebla is considered the baroque cradle of Mexico. The Cathedral, built in the Baroque style, is very popular among tourists. The municipal palace of Elizabethan architecture is located very close by. Sugar House (Casa del Alfenique) — another famous baroque building. Among other unusual and beautiful buildings, we note the Doll House.

If you want to buy handicrafts, especially talavera, head to the El Parian area. There are craft shops and markets here, and you will find many of the very beautiful authentic items for which Puebla is so famous.

Querétaro / Querétaro

Some consider Querétaro the most beautiful and most historical city in Mexico. In this city, the capital of the state of the same name, in addition to visiting the historical center, which is very beautiful and colorful, you will also need to visit Cerro de las Campana. This is the place where Maximoran of Habsburg was shot, and where the history of Mexico took a decisive turn towards final independence.

While walking along the streets of the historical center, we recommend eating baked cakes or delicious tamales. Just sit on a park bench in the park and watch the world spin — in Querétaro it is especially pleasant to do this.

San Luis Potosí / San Luis Potosí

San Luis Pfromosi can rightly be called synonymous with beautiful architecture. One of the oldest colonial cities in Mexico, it was once an important silver mining center and even the seat of government. This is a city that immediately captivates with its squares, gardens and churches. I want to return to the historical center again and again. San Luis Pfromosí has ​​more historical plazas than any other colonial city in Mexico. Plaza de Armas — you may visit the city square. Consider San Francisco Park with its University Walk.

If you have time, you can take a walk through Tangamanga Park, which, after Chapultepec, is one of the largest in Mexico. And if you are lucky enough to attend such an event as the projection of lights on the façade of the Templo del Carmen or Fiesta de Luz, the vivid impressions from the city will last for a long time!

San Miguel de Allende / San Miguel de Allende

Hometown of Independence hero Miguel Allende, it is located in the state of Guanajuato. When this state was the center of silver mining in Mexico, this is where the wealth and beauty of the local cities came from. Up to 90% of San Miguel de Allende's attractions are within a short walk from the city center. Don't miss the parish of San Miguel Arcangel (Church of St. Michael the Archangel) — from the highlight of the city, which is a must-see, and in addition to this, the Municipal Palace or the Temple of Oratory.

Walking around the center, you will notice that the dominant colors are yellow and ocher. This is one of the cities in Mexico where you can’t help but feel that it was “created for tourists,” the coloring of some of the buildings is so beautiful. It is one of those cities where a significant portion of the resident population is made up of non-residents, especially Americans.
