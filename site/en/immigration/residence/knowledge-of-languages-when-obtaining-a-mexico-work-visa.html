Knowledge of languages ​​when obtaining a Mexico work visa

When we tell clients the rules for obtaining a Mexico work visa, we always warn that knowledge of Spanish or English is extremely important. Whether you can pass an interview with the consul in one of these two languages ​​largely determines whether you will receive a work visa, and therefore a residence permit in Mexico.

In whatever country the consulate is located, the consul is not required to know the local language and conduct the interview in it. In addition, you may not know Spanish or English, but then the consul will have natural questions. You've never been to Mexico. How did you find an employer without knowing the language? How did you communicate and negotiate with him? How did you sign the papers for the job? Will you be able to perform your job functions without knowing the language?

The only consulate that allows interviews in Russian, — in Moscow.

You can come to the consular section with an interpreter. For example, the consular department in Costa Rica even recommends hiring their translator who speaks Russian. But firstly, not every consular department allows outsiders. Secondly, it’s better to mumble at an interview than to bring a translator and immediately show that you don’t know the language or aren’t even trying to speak it. Thirdly, if you have a good track record, if you outwardly give the impression of a respectable employee, the consul may not conduct an interview at all, but the presence of an interpreter can only worsen this impression.

Why do we warn in advance that knowing the language is so important? Because it is not us, not the employer and not the migration service, but only the Mexican consul who decides whether to give you a visa. To purchase a visa, we will provide a package of documents to which there will be no complaints. But you should understand that the result also depends on the impression you make on the consul.

What level of Spanish or English is required to successfully pass an interview at the Mexican consulate? No one will evaluate your language skills using the TOEFL, DELE or A1/B1 systems. For the Mexican consul, English is also not considered his native language, and he speaks it with the same pronunciation as you were taught in school, which makes it very easy to understand. Therefore, his level of language proficiency should be such that you understand the meaning of his questions and can answer them, even with errors. It would be worth mentioning that you will start learning Spanish in the near future.

What are the chances of getting a work visa without speaking Spanish or English? Quite low, let's say. No matter how much you are convinced otherwise, knowledge of languages ​​when obtaining a Mexican work visa has become extremely important in recent years. If 5 years ago Mexican employers were handing out job offers left and right, in recent years they have become more demanding in assessing candidates. Because the migration service has become much more demanding when providing work permits for foreigners, and it is not profitable for employers to contact the service for an incompetent candidate. This is a general trend that is also well known to consulates.

Another trend — an increase in the number of foreign workers from Latin American countries in Mexico. Most applications to consulates for work visas — precisely from them. They all know Spanish, and the appearance of a candidate among them without knowledge of the language arouses special attention from the consul. By the way, there are plenty of Americans too. Many American companies have offices in Mexico and relocate management and specialists from the United States.

And one last thing. When people ask us whether it's worth trying to get a Mexico work visa without knowing Spanish or English, we answer honestly: you won't necessarily get rejected, but it's best not to try. Therefore, the risk of not preparing a visa is quite high.
